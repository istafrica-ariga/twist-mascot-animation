# What the study mascot wears

## The rule, from the cape

The cape works because it **does the animating**. It is not decoration that says "superhero" — it
shows speed, direction, drag and follow-through. Take the cape off the flying mascot and the
flight reads as a floating doll.

So the test for any costume piece is not *does it look like studying*. It is:

> **Does it move, and does its movement tell you what the character feels?**

By that test the graduation cap fails twice. It is rigid, so it adds nothing to any take. And it
says *I have already finished, you have not* — which is a teacher, not the study buddy we settled
on.

## The candidates

### 1. A knitted scarf — the closest parallel to the cape

Same physics, opposite meaning. It trails, lags, settles. Nothing about it is academic, and every
child has one.

| beat | what the scarf does |
|---|---|
| idle | hangs, one slow sway |
| watching | still, pulled slightly in — the whole body goes quiet |
| yes | both ends fly up and out on the jump, settle two beats late |
| yes_big | ends whip right out, full follow-through |
| oops | ends drop dead straight, heavy |
| go_on | one end flicks up as the paw comes up |

The droop on `oops` is the thing. The body barely has to move — the scarf carries the whole
feeling, exactly the way the cape carries the flight.

### 2. An oversized hoodie — the strongest emotional range

Too big for the mascot, sleeves past the paws. Relatable on sight.

| beat | what the hoodie does |
|---|---|
| idle | sleeves hang long over the paws |
| watching | hood pushed back off the face |
| yes | sleeves flop and swing on the jump |
| yes_big | hood bounces off the back of the head |
| oops | **hood pulled up, half over the eyes** |
| go_on | hood pushed back off, face returns |

The hood up/down is a whole emotional axis on one object, and "hiding in your hood after getting
it wrong" is something a child recognises instantly. It is the single best idea here for *meaning*,
and the most work to draw.

### 3. A pencil — the cheapest legible signal

Behind the ear, or held. Says *working* in one shape, costs almost nothing to draw.

Tapped against the chin while watching, drops when the answer is wrong, spins once on a correct.
Too small to carry a take on its own, but it is the clearest "this character is studying" marker
of the three, and it combines with either of the others.

### 4. A sweatband — studying as training

Training, effort, practice rather than achievement. Kid-coded and un-academic. But it barely
moves, so it is costume, not animation — the same failure as the cap, just friendlier.

## Recommendation

**Scarf, plus a pencil behind the ear.**

The scarf because it is the cape's lesson applied exactly: one soft trailing object that does the
emotional work, especially the droop on a wrong answer. The pencil because the scarf alone says
*cosy*, not *studying*, and one small tool fixes that for almost nothing.

The hoodie is the better idea if we are willing to draw more — the hood-up beat is stronger than
anything the scarf can do. Worth choosing deliberately rather than by effort.

## Keeping it one character

The splash mascot wears a red cape. If the lesson mascot wears a scarf, make it **the same red**,
so it reads as the same character changing clothes rather than two mascots. That also gives a nice
line to use later: the cape is what it wears to fly, the scarf is what it wears to study.
